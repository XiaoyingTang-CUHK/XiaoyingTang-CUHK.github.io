Large Models (Reasoning, Prompt Tuning, Multimodal)

Our large-model research asks three questions: how to improve reasoning at lower cost, how to make generation more controllable and interpretable, and how to help agents use skills and workflows. For reasoning, G²RPO-A (ACL 2026) addresses the limited gains of GRPO on small models by injecting reference reasoning steps adaptively according to the training state, and AutoDeco (ICLR 2026) lets the model predict its own temperature and top-p at every step, bringing decoding into end-to-end training. For multimodal reasoning, Shape of Thought (ICML 2026) interleaves textual plans with rendered intermediate states so that every assembly step can be inspected, and TRACE (ICLR 2025) improves temporal grounding in video LLMs through causal event modeling. For prompt optimization and agents, aPSF (ACL 2026) discovers and factorizes the compositional structure of prompts, POES (NeurIPS 2026) cuts the evaluation cost of prompt optimization with submodular scheduling guarantees, and GoSkills and FlowSteer (NeurIPS 2026) study structured skill retrieval for agent skill libraries and agents that design their own workflows end to end. DynMoE (ICLR 2025) lets mixture-of-experts models adjust the number of active experts automatically during training.

These results appear at NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR and ACL. Code and models for several of them are released on the lab GitHub, and G²RPO-A, AutoDeco and TRACE have been featured by Synced (Jiqizhixin).

Representative Publications

  1. G²RPO-A: Guided Group Relative Policy Optimization with Adaptive Guidance ACL 2026Representative GRPO is an increasingly important RL method for improving LLM reasoning because it removes the need for costly critic models. However, its application to Small Language Models (SLMs) remains challenging due to limited initial reasoning ability and sparse reward signals. We propose G²RPO-A, the first adaptive guidance mechanism for GRPO that dynamically injects ground-truth reasoning tokens based on real-time training states. G²RPO-A consistently improves over vanilla GRPO and fixed-guidance baselines on mathematical reasoning and code generation benchmarks, enabling more effective RL training for 0.6B–1.7B models. The work demonstrates that adaptive guidance across the full difficulty spectrum is more effective than static filtering, providing a practical route toward efficient reasoning enhancement for smaller models.
  2. Shape of Thought: Progressive Object Assembly via Visual Chain-of-Thought ICML 2026Representative Shape of Thought (SoT) establishes a new paradigm for transparent, process-supervised compositional generation by introducing a visual Chain-of-Thought framework that enables multimodal models to perform progressive object assembly through interleaved textual plans and rendered intermediate 2D states, effectively overcoming the structural brittleness of text-only baselines in tasks like generative numeracy and attribute binding. To support this approach, the work contributes SoT-26K, a large-scale dataset of grounded assembly traces, and T2S-CompBench, a novel benchmark for evaluating structural integrity and reasoning-trace faithfulness. By making intermediate reasoning steps directly inspectable without relying on external geometric engines, SoT significantly outperforms text-only methods, marking a major leap toward interpretable, structurally sound AI reasoning.
  3. The End of Manual Decoding: Towards Truly End-to-End Language Models ICLR 2026 AutoDeco adds lightweight prediction heads to the Transformer that predict temperature and top-p from context at every step, and replaces hard truncation with a differentiable soft top-p, so that training and decoding become one end-to-end process. It matches or exceeds hand-tuned decoding across models and tasks with about 1.7% extra latency, and shows early ability to control decoding through natural-language instructions.
  4. Adaptive Prompt Structure Factorization: A Framework for Self-Discovering and Optimizing Compositional Prompt Programs ACL 2026 aPSF automatically discovers the compositional structure of a prompt and factorizes it into components that can be optimized separately, producing reusable prompt programs.
  5. Select Smarter, Not More: Prompt-Aware Evaluation Scheduling with Submodular Guarantees NeurIPS 2026 POES casts evaluation in automatic prompt optimization as online adaptive testing and combines an IRT-based discrimination utility, a facility-location coverage term and switching costs into a monotone submodular objective with a (1−1/e) greedy guarantee. Across 36 tasks from three benchmark families it reaches the highest average accuracy (6.2% above the best baseline) with about 4% token overhead, and evaluating on 20 examples matches or exceeds naive evaluation on 30–50, cutting token use by 35–60%.
  6. Group of Skills: Group-Structured Skill Retrieval for Agent Skill Libraries NeurIPS 2026 GoSkills changes what an agent retrieves from a flat skill list to a compact, role-labeled execution context. It builds anchor-centered skill groups from a typed skill graph and renders a fixed execution contract with Start, Support, Check and Avoid fields under a small skill budget, without changing the downstream agent or environment. On SkillsBench and ALFWorld it improves over flat skill-access baselines.
  7. FlowSteer: Towards Agents Designing Agentic Workflows via Reinforced Progressive Canvas Editing NeurIPS 2026 FlowSteer proposes a paradigm in which a single agent designs, end to end, the workflow that a downstream executor runs. An executable Workflow Canvas returns syntax-checked execution feedback for every atomic edit, and a lightweight policy agent issues one edit per turn and is trained with reinforcement learning. The framework supports diverse operator libraries and interchangeable LLM backends and outperforms baselines on twelve datasets.
  8. Dynamic Mixture of Experts: An Auto-Tuning Approach for Efficient Transformer Models ICLR 2025 DynMoE introduces top-any gating, which lets each token decide how many experts to activate, and an adaptive process that adjusts the number of experts during training, removing manual tuning of these mixture-of-experts hyperparameters.
  9. TRACE: Temporal Grounding Video LLM via Causal Event Modeling ICLR 2025 TRACE represents a video as a sequence of events and predicts the current event from previous events, the instruction and the video input, improving temporal grounding in video LLMs. It has received over 100 Google Scholar citations.

Intelligent EV Charging Optimization

Transportation electrification creates new problems for grid stability, traffic congestion and charging-station operation. The lab develops optimization, game-theoretic and machine-learning methods for EV charging and battery-swapping networks that balance grid security, user experience and operator revenue. On operation and pricing, we study joint pricing and charging for non-cooperative battery swapping stations in a competitive market (IEEE TMC), privacy-preserving pricing and scheduling that offloads computation to EVs (IEEE TSG), and personalized federated management and load balancing across stations (IEEE TII). On planning, we determine when a station should upgrade from slow to fast charging (IEEE TTE). On user behavior, we were the first to combine prospect theory with a Bayesian game to model range anxiety and risk aversion in highway charging. Our work on strategic V2G trading received the IEEE SmartGridComm 2024 Best Paper Award.

The lab's vehicle-grid interaction algorithms support China's largest vehicle-grid engineering demonstration, spanning 5 provinces and 63 cities with more than 100,000 EVs, which was featured by CCTV and People's Daily (see the CCTV News report). Prof. Tang has also contributed to IEEE Standards P3436 and P3334, Shenzhen Local Standard DB4403, and China's first Vehicle-to-Grid White Paper.

Representative Publications

  1. Optimal Pricing and Charging Strategy Design for Non-Cooperative Battery Swapping Stations IEEE TMC 2024Representative Pricing competition and charging control are tightly coupled in battery-swapping markets, yet they are often optimized separately. This work develops the first hierarchical non-cooperative game that jointly optimizes day-ahead pricing and real-time charging decisions, and proves the existence and uniqueness of the resulting subgame-perfect Nash equilibrium. The analysis provides a rigorous foundation for decentralized operation among competing stations under uncertain demand.
  2. Economical Analysis of Optimal Charging Station Upgrade Time IEEE TTE 2026Representative Existing studies lack a rigorous rule for determining when a charging station should upgrade from slow to fast charging technology. This work develops a unified economic model covering static and dynamic demand as well as monopoly and duopoly competition, and proves the existence of a unique optimal upgrade threshold. The theoretical analysis reveals how technology cost, demand growth, and competitive pressure jointly determine the optimal timing.
  3. Incorporating Bounded Rationality Into Electric Vehicle Highway Charging Decisions: A Bayesian Game Analysis IEEE Internet of Things Journal, 2025Representative Conventional highway-charging models assume perfectly rational drivers and therefore overlook range anxiety and risk-sensitive behavior. This work is the first to integrate prospect theory with a Bayesian game to jointly model range anxiety, risk aversion, and incomplete information. It further proves the existence and uniqueness of the Bayesian Nash equilibrium, making behavioral uncertainty mathematically tractable.
  4. Personalized Federated Management and Load Balancing for Multiple Charging Stations IEEE TII 2025Representative This work develops the first hierarchical personalized federated reinforcement-learning framework for coordinating multiple charging stations with heterogeneous objectives, without sharing raw local data. By combining similarity-based personalized aggregation with retailer–station coordination, it balances station-level performance and system-wide load management in a unified framework. The design supports scalable, privacy-preserving deployment in practical multi-station charging networks.
  5. Charging Station Pricing and Scheduling With Localized Personal Information via Offloading Computation to EVs IEEE TSG 2026 This work proposes Master-Follower Collaborative Reinforcement Learning (MFCRL) for cooperative operation of multiple charging stations. The stations act as masters and offload training tasks that involve private internal states to the EVs, which act as followers, then aggregate their local model updates. The trained models adapt to dynamic electricity prices and variable station occupancy while protecting EV owners' privacy.

Federated Learning

Federated learning lets many clients train a model together without sharing their raw data. The lab studies its theoretical foundations under non-convex objectives, data heterogeneity and resource constraints, targeting accuracy, convergence, fairness and robustness. For client sampling, DELTA (NeurIPS 2023) gives a provably optimal unbiased sampling scheme in the non-convex setting. For data heterogeneity, FedBR (ICML 2023) reduces bias in local feature learning and classifier updates. For distribution shifts, FedRC (ICML 2024) is the first to handle feature, label and concept shifts at the same time, and HCFL+ (ICLR 2025) integrates and improves clustered federated learning methods. For fairness, FedEBA+ (ICML 2026) derives closed-form fair aggregation weights from the maximum-entropy principle and improves fairness without losing global accuracy. For the decentralized setting, AFIND+ (IEEE TMC 2026) helps each client find helpful neighbors and decide adaptively whom to collaborate with.

DELTA has been cited by follow-up work at INFOCOM, ICLR and NeurIPS, FedRC has been adopted as a baseline in later NeurIPS work, and the code of FedEBA+ is available on the lab GitHub.

Representative Publications

  1. DELTA: Diverse Client Sampling for Fasting Federated Learning NeurIPS 2023Representative This paper addresses a fundamental challenge in Federated Learning (FL): identifying the optimal client sampling strategy for nonconvex settings. We establish a new convergence-analysis paradigm for general unbiased client sampling and propose DELTA, the first diverse gradient-based unbiased sampler. We prove that DELTA achieves superior convergence rates compared to existing state-of-the-art methods, including classical importance sampling (FedIS). We also derive a novel, tighter convergence analysis for nonconvex FedIS, establishing an improved convergence rate bound under significantly milder assumptions compared to existing studies. These results fundamentally solve the key theoretical barriers in nonconvex FL convergence analysis, help close the critical gap in FL sampling theory, and effectively address the longstanding challenge in the field.
  2. FedEBA+: Towards Fair and Effective Federated Learning via Entropy-Based Model ICML 2026Representative We introduce FedEBA+, a novel framework that resolves the fundamental tension between client fairness and global performance (accuracy) in Federated Learning. By leveraging the maximum-entropy principle for unbiased client aggregation and step-wise alignment to correct local drift, FedEBA+ simultaneously maximizes global performance and minimizes client-level disparity, surpassing prior methods that often sacrifice accuracy for fairness. Theoretically, this work breaks new ground as the first to extend federated fairness analysis from restricted linear models to general strongly convex settings, while establishing comparable convergence rates in nonconvex regimes. This work offers a comprehensive theoretical foundation for fair and effective federated learning in realistic, complex scenarios.
  3. FedRC: Tackling Diverse Distribution Shifts Challenge in Federated Learning by Robust Clustering ICML 2024Representative Unlike prior work, which typically addresses isolated shifts (e.g., feature or label shifts in isolation), FedRC is the first method to tackle the simultaneous occurrence of feature, label, and concept shifts—a pervasive yet previously underexplored challenge in Federated Learning (FL). FedRC integrates a bi-level optimization framework with a novel objective function, enabling robust clustering despite the complexities of diverse distribution shifts. This innovation ensures both stability and scalability for real-world FL deployments.
  4. FedBR: Improving Federated Learning on Heterogeneous Data via Local Learning Bias Reduction ICML 2023Representative This work identifies three under-explored sources of biased local learning in heterogeneous federated learning and proposes FedBR, the first unified framework that reduces bias in both local feature learning and classifier updates through output balancing and min–max contrastive learning.
  5. Enhancing Clustered Federated Learning: Integration of Strategies and Improved Methodologies ICLR 2025 This work unifies existing clustered federated learning methods in one holistic framework, analyzes where they fall short, and proposes the improved algorithm HCFL+.
  6. AFIND+: Adaptive Client-Centric Collaboration for Decentralized Personalized Federated Learning IEEE TMC 2026 (accepted; link to preprint) In decentralized federated learning there is no central server, and not every neighbor helps a client's local model. AFIND+ identifies helpful neighbors, adaptively adjusts how many to select, and aggregates their models according to their contributions. On real-world datasets with diverse data partitions it outperforms other decentralized sampling algorithms and works with most existing decentralized optimization algorithms.

Computing-Energy Co-optimization

The growth of AI drives fast growth in data-center electricity demand, which makes the coordination of computing and power a new research problem. The lab studies data-center–grid co-optimization and the coupling between AI workload scheduling and grid dispatch. This direction builds on the lab's power-system research. For frequency regulation, we designed a primary frequency regulation market for converter-integrated power systems that pays each unit according to its combined contribution to frequency nadir, rate of change of frequency and regulation mileage (IEEE TPWRS). For scheduling, we brought multiple reserve types into the unit commitment problem and solved it with a hybrid of reinforcement learning and optimization (IEEE TII).
